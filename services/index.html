Somatic

Somatic Healing & Support

Somatic work offers a gentle, body-centered space to slow down, reconnect with yourself, and develop a deeper awareness of what is happening within you.

My approach draws on somatic practices and an understanding of the nervous system, including polyvagal theory. The vagus nerve connects the brain with the heart, lungs, and digestive system and plays an important role in how we move between states of alertness, connection, and rest.

Through gentle touch, attention, and intentional pauses, we create space to listen to the body without forcing an outcome. Some people experience a sense of calm, emotional release, or a greater connection with themselves. Each person's experience is different, and there is no particular result you need to achieve.

What to Expect

We begin with a conversation about how you are arriving and what you would like support with. Any touch is discussed and agreed upon beforehand. You remain in control throughout the session and can change your mind, pause, or stop at any time.

Somatic sessions are offered as supportive, body-centered work. They are not a substitute for medical care or psychotherapy, and I do not diagnose or treat medical or mental health conditions.

Please share relevant health history, current treatment, and any previous experiences with touch that may help me understand how best to support you.

  • Duration: Approximately 2 hours

Ceremonial

Kambo Ceremonies

Kambo, also known as sapo, is a traditional Amazonian medicine derived from the secretion of the giant monkey frog (Phyllomedusa bicolor). It has been carried through generations by Indigenous peoples of the Amazon and holds an important place within several ancestral traditions.

My approach to Kambo is grounded in respect for the medicine, its cultural roots, and the responsibility of sitting with it. Kambo is an ordeal medicine: its physical intensity asks for preparation, honesty, and a willingness to meet the experience with care.

Traditional Roots

The frog Phyllomedusa bicolor lives in the Amazon rainforest, and its medicine has been held for generations by Indigenous peoples, particularly Panoan-speaking peoples such as the Matsés, Huni Kuin, and Yawanawá. Each people has its own traditions, stories, and ways of relating to the medicine. I am not a member of these communities, and I share this brief overview with respect for their distinct cultural lineages.

  • Matsés: The Matsés live in the Javari River region of the Peruvian and Brazilian Amazon. Traditionally, Kambo has been used by hunters in connection with sharpening the senses and preparing for the hunt.
  • Huni Kuin: The Huni Kuin live in Acre, Brazil. Within their traditions, Kambo has been associated with strength, good fortune in the hunt, and the release of a heaviness of body and spirit known as panema.
  • Yawanawá: The Yawanawá of Acre, Brazil, are also known for their traditional relationship with Kambo. Their leaders have spoken about the importance of respecting the medicine and the ways in which it is shared beyond their communities.

These traditions belong to specific peoples and cultural contexts, and they should not be treated as interchangeable. As Kambo has spread beyond the Amazon, Indigenous voices have emphasized the importance of respect, permission, and learning from the communities who have carried this medicine across generations.

I hold this responsibility close and approach the work with humility, honoring the medicine's ancestral roots while acknowledging the limits of my own perspective.

What to Expect

A Kambo ceremony generally lasts 2 to 3 hours. We begin with a conversation about how you are arriving, your intentions, and any questions you may have. The ceremony may include prayer, smudging, or other elements to open the space.

Kambo is traditionally applied through small points on the skin. The experience can bring heat, a rapid heartbeat, flushing, sweating, swelling, nausea, and vomiting. The more intense phase often lasts around 20 to 40 minutes, followed by time to rest and recover. I remain present with you throughout the process.

Please wear loose-fitting clothing. You are welcome to bring personal items for the altar that feel meaningful to you.

Once the ceremony is complete, tea and fresh fruit will be offered as you rest and gently transition out of the experience.

Preparation, Honesty & Suitability

Kambo is an ordeal medicine and is not appropriate for everyone. A full intake and an honest conversation with me will help determine whether it is a good fit for you.

In honor of the medicine and your own body, transparency about your health history, medications, and current circumstances is of the utmost importance. The preparation dieta and all guidelines provided beforehand are an essential part of the process. Serious complications can occur if these guidelines and the preparation dieta are not followed.

Please approach the preparation with care and communicate openly with me about anything that may affect your participation. Once your intake has been reviewed, I will share the preparation guidance and answer any remaining questions.

Sananga

Sananga is a traditional Amazonian plant medicine made from plants in the Tabernaemontana family. Indigenous communities have traditionally used these eye drops in different ceremonial and practical contexts, including as part of preparation for hunting in the jungle.

The drops can produce a strong burning or stinging sensation, often accompanied by watery eyes. This typically passes, and I will be present to support you through the experience.

Traditionally, Sananga is associated with sharpening vision and perception. In a ceremonial setting, people may also work with it to support meditative states, settle the mind, and invite a more restful state.

Each person's experience is different. We will talk beforehand about your intentions and whether this offering feels appropriate for you.

Please let me know about any eye conditions, infection, recent injury or surgery, or glaucoma before considering Sananga. Contact lenses should be removed beforehand. If significant pain or changes in vision persist after use, seek medical attention.

Rapé / Hapé

Rapé, also spelled Hapé, is a traditional Amazonian snuff made from finely ground plant materials. When administered by another person, it is delivered using a tepi, a small applicator designed to direct the medicine into the nostrils. It holds an important place in the ceremonial and spiritual practices of various Indigenous communities of the Amazon.

I offer a variety of Rapé blends, each with its own qualities and traditional associations. We can discuss the available blends beforehand and choose one that feels aligned with your intentions and circumstances.

Rapé can be a companion for meditation, grounding, prayer, and bringing focused attention to the present moment. It offers an opportunity to pause, reconnect with yourself, and create space for reflection.

The experience can be physically strong. Watery eyes and nose, dizziness, nausea, or occasionally vomiting may occur.

Rapé contains tobacco, so we will discuss your health and circumstances during the discovery call to determine whether it is an appropriate offering for you.

  • Duration: Approximately 30 to 60 minutes
  • Contribution: $300 MXN
